Rental property consulting in Denton County, Texas

Hourly consulting for rental owners and buyers in Denton County, including Lewisville, Flower Mound, Little Elm, and Denton.

Reviewed by Denver McClure, founder of Apollo Advising. Updated October 12, 2026.

Short answer. Denton County mixes established suburbs, fast-growing communities with MUD taxes, lake-area homes with short-term rental appeal, and student housing near the universities in Denton. The right strategy depends heavily on the submarket, so the questions we see most are which rental strategy fits a specific property, what a MUD tax does to cash flow, and how to price a lake-area or student rental realistically.

What is different about Denton County rentals

Municipal utility districts

MUDs are common in newer developments, especially in unincorporated and fast-growing areas. Their taxes can add meaningfully to the total rate. Always use the full rate for the exact address.

Lake-area rentals

Homes near Lewisville Lake attract short-term and seasonal guests. Check city or county rules, HOA restrictions, and the insurance cost before counting on short-term income.

Student rentals in Denton

Rentals near the University of North Texas and Texas Woman's University lease on an academic calendar. Expect more turnover, by-the-room leasing questions, and wear, and check city occupancy rules.

Established suburbs

In Lewisville, Flower Mound, and Highland Village, many owners bought years ago and now face the hold, sell, or exchange question on a property that has appreciated well.

Property taxes

Rental property in Denton County is appraised by the Denton Central Appraisal District. Rentals do not get the homestead exemption or the 10% homestead cap, and the separate 20% cap on non-homestead property is set to expire after the 2026 tax year. Protest by May 15 or 30 days after your notice arrives, whichever is later. Our protest guide covers the evidence that tends to work.
