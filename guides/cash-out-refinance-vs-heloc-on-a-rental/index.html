Cash-out refinance or HELOC on a rental?

Cash-out refinance or HELOC on a rental property? How each one works on an investment property, what they cost, and the cash flow test to run before you borrow.

By Denver McClure, founder of Apollo Advising. Updated October 12, 2026.

Short answer. A cash-out refinance replaces your whole mortgage with a bigger one, so it reprices your existing balance at today's rate. A HELOC leaves your current loan alone and adds a separate, usually variable-rate line. If your current rate is low, a HELOC or a smaller second loan is often cheaper per dollar of cash. Fewer lenders offer HELOCs on rentals, so compare what is actually available.

The hidden cost of a cash-out refinance

Say the rental is worth $400,000 and you owe $150,000 at 3.5%. A cash-out refinance to 75% of value is a $300,000 loan, giving you about $150,000 before closing costs. At 7.25%, first-year interest is about $21,750, compared with about $5,250 on the old loan.

So the new $150,000 effectively costs $16,500 a year in added interest, about 11.0% on the cash you received, because the old balance was repriced too. A HELOC at 8.5% on a $130,000 line costs about $11,050 a year if fully drawn, and nothing on what you leave undrawn.

These are simplified, interest-only figures for comparison. Real costs depend on fees, terms, and how quickly you repay.

The test to run first

Before you borrow against a rental, answer three questions:

  1. Does the rental still cash-flow with the higher payment, after every cost and a reserve?
  2. Does the use of the money earn more, after tax and with honest risk, than the borrowing costs? Paying off higher-rate debt or funding a renovation that raises rent are different from speculating.
  3. If rates rise or the rental sits vacant, can you carry the payment from other income?

If you are pulling equity because the property's return on equity has fallen, also compare borrowing with selling or exchanging, since those are the other ways to put trapped equity to work.

A Texas note

Texas has special rules for home equity loans on a homestead, including limits on how much can be borrowed. Those rules protect primary residences, not rental properties, so rentals are generally financed under ordinary lending rules. Confirm with your lender.
