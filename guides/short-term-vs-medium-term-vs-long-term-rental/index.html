Airbnb, medium-term, or long-term: comparing what you keep

How short-term, furnished medium-term, and long-term rentals compare on costs, time, and rules, with a worked example on the same house.

By Denver McClure, founder of Apollo Advising. Updated October 12, 2026.

Short answer. Compare the three on net income after every cost and on the hours each one takes. Short-term rentals usually bring in the most gross revenue and carry the highest costs and risk. Long-term leases bring in the least and cost the least to run. Furnished medium-term stays of 30 days or more often land in between.

A worked example

Here is one house run three ways. The numbers are illustrative, not a forecast for any market. Mortgage, property tax, and most insurance are left out because they are roughly the same in all three, though insurance for a short-term rental often costs more.

Assumes short-term at $180 a night, 60% occupancy; medium-term at $3,000 a month, 10 months booked; long-term at $2,200 a month, 11.5 months leased. Maintenance and the CapEx reserve start at 10% and 5% of rent. Hotel occupancy tax (13% here) is added to each short-term guest’s bill, so it is not counted as a cost; if your nightly rate already includes it, it comes out of what you keep.

The short-term rental brings in about $14,120 more a year than the lease. After cleaning, fees, management, maintenance, and a reserve for capital items, it keeps $5,058 less, and it took the most time and carried the most risk. Change the occupancy, the nightly rate, or the market and the ranking can flip, which is exactly why the comparison has to be built for your property.

What to check before you switch

  • City ordinances, permits, and occupancy taxes for stays under 30 days
  • HOA rules and your insurance policy
  • Furnishing cost and how long it takes to earn back
  • Demand for 30-day stays nearby: hospitals, corporate offices, and insurance housing
  • How many hours a week you are willing to give it
