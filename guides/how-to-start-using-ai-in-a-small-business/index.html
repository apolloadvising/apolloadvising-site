How to start using AI in a small business

A practical order of operations for owners: where AI pays off first, what to set up, what to avoid, and how to keep your data and customers safe.

By Denver McClure, founder of Apollo Advising. Updated October 12, 2026.

Short answer. Start with one costly, repetitive task, not a company-wide rollout. For most owners that is lead response, follow-up, or a weekly report. Set it up in accounts you own, keep a person approving anything customers see, measure the time it saves for a month, then add the next task.

Pick the task that costs you the most

List the work that eats your week and the leads or money that slip through. The best first project is repetitive, rule-based, and expensive when it is missed: replying to new leads, following up on estimates, chasing invoices, or pulling the weekly numbers together.

Use the tools you already pay for

Most businesses already have a phone platform, a CRM or spreadsheet, accounting software, and email. Many of these now include AI features or connect to AI assistants such as Claude or Grok. Start there before buying anything new.

Keep ownership and data in your hands

Set tools up in your own accounts, not a consultant's, so they keep running if the relationship ends. Read each provider's data terms, avoid pasting sensitive customer data into tools that do not protect it, and remove access for anyone who no longer needs it.

Keep a person in the loop

AI can be wrong. Let it draft, sort, and summarize, and have a person approve anything that goes to a customer until you trust the results. Rules on calls, texts, recordings, and email marketing still apply when AI does the work.

Measure, then expand

Track one number for a month: hours saved, response time, or leads followed up. When the first task works, add the next one. Owners who build step by step end up with systems their team actually uses.

Common questions

What is the easiest AI win for a small business?

Faster lead response and follow-up. It is simple to set up, easy to measure, and directly tied to revenue.

Do I need to hire a developer?

Often not. Many owners can set up their first AI workflows with one-on-one help and then run them themselves.
