DSCR loans for rental property, explained

How DSCR loans for rental property work: how lenders calculate the ratio, what the loans usually cost compared with conventional mortgages, and when one fits.

By Denver McClure, founder of Apollo Advising. Updated October 12, 2026.

Short answer. A DSCR loan qualifies you based on the property's rent instead of your personal income. Lenders divide the monthly rent by the monthly payment including taxes, insurance, and any HOA dues. Many want a ratio of at least 1.0 to 1.25. The tradeoff is usually a higher rate, a larger down payment, and often a prepayment penalty compared with a conventional loan.

How the ratio is calculated

DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio. For these loans, most lenders use gross monthly rent, from a lease or an appraiser's rent estimate, divided by the full monthly housing payment: principal, interest, property tax, insurance, and HOA dues. A ratio of 1.0 means the rent exactly covers the payment.

An example

A house renting for $2,400 a month, with a $240,000 loan at 7.5% over 30 years. Principal and interest are about $1,678. Add $600 of property tax and $220 of insurance and the payment is about $2,498, for a DSCR of 0.96.

To reach 1.25 on this rent, the payment would need to be about $1,920 or less, which usually means a bigger down payment or a lower price.

Notice what the ratio leaves out: vacancy, repairs, management, and capital reserves. A property can qualify for a DSCR loan and still lose money every month once those are counted.

Terms vary widely by lender and change with the market, so treat this as a map of the questions to ask, not a rate sheet.

When a DSCR loan tends to fit

  • Self-employed owners whose tax returns understate their income after deductions
  • Investors who have reached the limit on conventional financed properties
  • Buyers who want to hold title in an LLC from the start
  • Properties with strong rent relative to price

They tend to fit poorly when the rent barely covers the payment, when you might sell or refinance within a few years and trigger a prepayment penalty, or when you qualify easily for a conventional loan at a lower rate.
